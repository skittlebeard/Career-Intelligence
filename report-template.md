## Executive summary

## Ranked verified opportunities
Use compact opportunity cards or a table with at most five columns: Priority | Employer and position | Location/work arrangement | Relevance score | View job posting.
Use active employer/ATS hyperlinks. Under each entry retain verification UTC date/time, job ID, work arrangement, published compensation or “Not disclosed”, confidence, scoring coverage, contact status, and a brief fit rationale. Keep unknown constraints visible as items requiring confirmation.

## Opportunity scorecards
Include an editable scorecard for every verified eligible opening with full dimension labels: Role and capability fit (40%); Company and initiative relevance (25%); Relationship accessibility (15%); Career and practical alignment (20%). Show raw score out of 100, weighted contribution, and a concise reason for each. Show overall or provisional score, percentage of scoring information available, and evidence confidence separately. Explain missing information and its effect on comparison. Missing contacts or compensation remain Unknown, not zero. Preserve the rubric's missing-subdimension treatment and keep detailed calculation evidence in the appendix. Scores express relevance, not probability of being hired.
Use separate clearly marked provisional rankings for incomplete scores.

## Research coverage and exclusions
Companies screened but lacking verified roles; unverified postings excluded; sources searched and access limitations.

## Change log vs previous run
New to research; changed; still open; no longer verified; confirmed closed (with proof). Omit when no previous Word report or optional snapshot is supplied. Keep canonical job URLs, job IDs where available, verification dates and scores in this Word report so it can support the next comparison without JSON.

## Methodology and scoring appendix
40/25/15/20 weighting; confidence; missing-data treatment; research limits; sources and timestamps.

## Format and usability
Deliver one editable Word (.docx) document by default, not this Markdown source template. Use US Letter portrait, readable 10.5–11 pt body text, consistent Word heading styles, page numbers, restrained color, and generous spacing. All text, scores and tables must remain editable. Aim for one or two readable pages per detailed brief without omitting material evidence. Keep the main summary concise; avoid an eleven-column table that cannot fit comfortably on a page.

Each brief must include an active “View job posting” hyperlink and a copyable canonical URL; retain exact title, employer, verification UTC time and job ID. Explain fit using specific resume evidence; show requirements, supported strengths, transferable strengths, gaps, and claims the candidate cannot substantiate. Separate confirmed contacts from public unconnected stakeholders. Include 3–5 research-informed interview questions and suggested next steps for the candidate; do not imply applications or outreach were performed.

Use numbered, clickable source notes with publication dates when known and actual observation dates. Use hyperlinks that work outside the originating AI platform, not host-only citation tokens. Label hypotheses and alternative explanations clearly. Render and inspect the document where supported, verify hyperlinks, and fix layout errors before delivery. End the chat with a brief summary and the Word download link. If Word generation is unavailable, explain that limit and return readable content in chat. Produce Markdown or JSON exports only on explicit request.
